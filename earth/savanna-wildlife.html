01 — Overview

Savannas, in
a nutshell.

Grasslands, savannas and steppes stretch from the Serengeti to the Mongolian steppe and the North American prairie. Seasonal rains, fire and grazing animals keep these open landscapes alive.

02 — Residents

Grazers, hunters
& scavengers.

Grazers

Millions of grazers keep grass short, spread seeds and fertilise the soil.

Browsers

Tall browsers shape trees and open up space for other species.

Predators

Hunters keep herds moving and healthy, which protects the grass itself.

Scavengers

Nature’s clean-up crew, though many vulture species are now critically endangered.

03 — Spotlight

Open-country
icons at risk.

Vulnerable

Cheetah

Fewer than about 7,000 remain, squeezed by lost prey and land.

Endangered

African wild dog

Highly social hunters, down to roughly 6,000 animals in scattered packs.

Near threatened

Saiga antelope

A steppe survivor that rebounded strongly after a population crash.

“The horizon is the habitat.”

04 — Pressures

What is
changing.

01

Farmland conversion

Grasslands are among the most converted and least protected habitats on Earth.

02

Fences & roads

Barriers cut migration routes that animals have followed for thousands of years.

03

Poaching & snares

Illegal hunting removes animals faster than they can breed.
