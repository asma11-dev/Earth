01 — Overview

South America, in
a nutshell.

South America is a land of extremes: the Amazon, the largest rainforest on Earth, the Andes, the driest desert at Atacama and glaciers in Patagonia. It is one of the most biodiverse regions in the world.

02 — Landscapes

Places that
define South America.

The Amazon

A rainforest of about 5.5 million km² that stores huge amounts of carbon and shapes global weather.

Machu Picchu

A 15th-century Inca citadel perched high in the Andes of Peru.

Patagonia

Granite spires, glaciers and windswept steppe at the southern tip of the continent.

03 — Wildlife

Life on
the edge.

Near threatened

Jaguar

The Americas’ largest cat needs vast, connected forest to hunt and roam.

Endangered

Amazon river dolphin

The pink boto faces dams, fishing nets and pollution.

Vulnerable

Andean condor

One of the largest flying birds, with wings up to 3 metres across.

“The lungs of the planet breathe here.”

04 — Challenges

What is
at stake.

01

Deforestation

Fires and clearing for cattle and crops have removed a sizeable share of the Amazon’s forest.

02

Retreating glaciers

Andean glaciers have lost large areas, threatening water for cities like Lima and La Paz.

03

Illegal mining

Gold mining pollutes rivers with mercury and harms Indigenous communities.
