01 — Overview

Extinction Crisis, in
a nutshell.

The IUCN Red List tracks how close species are to extinction. More than 45,000 of over 160,000 assessed species are threatened. Scientists estimate up to a million species face extinction, but careful protection has also brought real comebacks.

02 — Red List

Where is
each species?

Driver 1

Habitat loss

Farming, logging and cities remove the places wildlife needs. It is the biggest threat on land.

Driver 2

Overexploitation

Hunting, fishing and the wildlife trade take animals faster than they recover.

Driver 3

Climate change

Shifting temperatures and rainfall push species out of the habitats they know.

Driver 4

Pollution

Plastic, pesticides and chemicals poison food chains.

Driver 5

Invasive species

Introduced predators and diseases devastate native wildlife, especially on islands.

03 — Comebacks

Proof it
can work.

Recovered

Humpback whale

Numbers have rebounded strongly since commercial whaling was banned.

Downlisted

Giant panda

Moved from Endangered to Vulnerable in 2016 after habitat protection.

Downlisted

Iberian lynx

04 — Solutions

What it
takes.

01

Protect 30% by 2030

In 2022 governments agreed on a global goal to protect at least 30% of land and sea by 2030.

02

Restore & connect habitat

Wildlife corridors and replanted forests help populations recover and move.

03

Local and Indigenous stewardship

Communities who live with nature are often its best guardians, and need support and rights.
