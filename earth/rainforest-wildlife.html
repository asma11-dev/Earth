01 — Overview

Rainforests, in
a nutshell.

Tropical rainforests cover only about 6% of Earth’s land, yet they shelter an enormous share of its plants and animals across the Amazon, the Congo Basin and Southeast Asia. Each layer, from forest floor to treetop, supports different life.

02 — Layers

Four floors
of life.

Up to ~60 m

Emergent layer

The tallest trees poke above the canopy, home to harpy eagles, monkeys and countless insects.

Most life lives here

Canopy

A dense green roof of leaves, flowers and fruit feeding toucans, sloths, monkeys and tree frogs.

Dim and humid

Understory

Shade-loving plants, snakes, jaguars and leopards move through the half-light.

About 2% sunlight

Forest floor

Leaf litter, fungi and insects recycle nutrients while tapirs and okapi forage.

03 — Spotlight

Forest giants
in trouble.

Critically endangered

Bornean orangutan

Habitat loss for plantations has cut their numbers by more than half over decades.

Endangered

Okapi

The shy “forest giraffe” of the Congo is threatened by hunting and logging.

Vulnerable

Harpy eagle

One of the world’s most powerful eagles, needing huge areas of intact forest.

“Every leaf here is somebody’s home.”

04 — Pressures

What is
changing.

01

Deforestation

About 10 million hectares of forest are cleared worldwide each year, much of it for farming and pasture.

02

Fragmentation & fire

Roads and clearing split habitat into patches too small for wide-ranging animals.

03

Wildlife trade

Illegal hunting and trapping empty forests of animals even where trees still stand.
