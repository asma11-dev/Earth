01 — Overview

Africa, in
a nutshell.

Africa is the cradle of humankind and the world’s second-largest continent: 54 countries, more than 2,000 languages and landscapes that run from the Sahara to the Congo Basin. Its wildlife, rivers and rainforests are among the most precious on Earth.

02 — Landscapes

Places that
define Africa.

The Sahara

The largest hot desert on Earth covers about 9 million km², with dunes, mountains and ancient rock art.

The Serengeti

Home to the great migration, when more than a million wildebeest follow the rains across Tanzania and Kenya.

Victoria Falls

A curtain of water on the Zambezi, about 1.7 km wide, known locally as “the smoke that thunders”.

03 — Wildlife

Life on
the edge.

Endangered

African elephant

Savanna elephants are Endangered and forest elephants Critically Endangered, mainly from poaching and lost habitat.

Endangered

Mountain gorilla

Just over 1,000 remain in the wild, a rare conservation success story built on community-led protection.

Critically endangered

Black rhino

Poaching pushed numbers down to a few thousand; strict protection has slowly helped them recover.

“Every story of us begins here.”

04 — Challenges

What is
at stake.

01

Desertification & drought

The Sahel is losing farmland to drying soils. Projects like the Great Green Wall aim to restore degraded land.

02

Poaching & habitat loss

Growing populations and trade in wildlife products squeeze the space left for animals.

03

Climate injustice

Africa produces only a small share of global emissions, yet is among the regions most exposed to floods, heat and drought.
