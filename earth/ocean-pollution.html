01 — Overview

Ocean Pollution, in
a nutshell.

Most marine pollution starts on land. Plastic, farm runoff, oil and lost fishing gear travel through rivers and drains into the sea, where they harm wildlife, enter the food chain and damage ecosystems for decades.

02 — Sources

Where it
comes from.

Plastic waste

Bags, bottles and packaging break into microplastics that never fully disappear.

Farm & sewage runoff

Excess nutrients feed algae blooms that use up oxygen and create dead zones.

Oil & chemicals

Spills, shipping and industrial discharge poison water and coastlines.

Lost fishing gear

Abandoned nets keep trapping turtles, whales and fish for years as “ghost gear”.

03 — Impact

Who pays
the price.

Impact

Sea turtles

Turtles often mistake floating plastic bags for jellyfish and can be harmed when they eat them.

Impact

Seabirds

Most seabird species have been found with plastic in their stomachs, including chicks fed by their parents.

Impact

People

Microplastics have been found in seafood, salt and drinking water, and research on health effects continues.

“There is no “away”. Every piece of plastic is still somewhere.”

04 — Solutions

What is
working.

01

Bans & deposit schemes

Many countries now restrict single-use plastics, and bottle deposit systems recover very high shares of containers.

02

Cleaning rivers

Barriers and interceptors catch waste in rivers before it reaches the sea.

03

A global treaty

In 2022 UN member states agreed to negotiate a legally binding agreement on plastic pollution.
