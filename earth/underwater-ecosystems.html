01 — Overview

Underwater Ecosystems, in
a nutshell.

Beneath the surface are ecosystems as rich as any rainforest: coral reefs, kelp forests, mangroves, seagrass meadows, hydrothermal vents and polar seas. Each one shelters wildlife, protects coastlines and stores carbon.

02 — Habitats

Five worlds,
one ocean.

Coral reefs

Built by tiny animals over thousands of years, reefs protect coasts and feed millions of people.

Kelp forests

Towering underwater forests along cold coasts shelter fish, otters and urchins.

Mangroves & seagrass

Coastal nurseries that trap carbon, filter water and buffer storms.

Hydrothermal vents

Deep-sea chimneys where life runs on chemicals, not sunlight.

Polar seas

Cold, rich waters where krill feed penguins, seals and whales.

04 — Pressures

What puts them
at risk.

01

Coral bleaching

Marine heatwaves cause corals to expel the algae that feed them, and repeated events can kill whole reefs.

02

Mangrove loss

About a fifth of mangroves have been lost since 1980 to farming, shrimp ponds and development.

03

Deep-sea mining

Proposals to mine the seabed worry scientists, who say slow-growing deep ecosystems could take centuries to recover.
