01 — Overview

Asia, in
a nutshell.

Asia is the largest and most populous continent, home to roughly six in ten people on Earth. It holds the Himalaya, the deepest lake, vast steppes, monsoon forests and some of the world’s oldest cities.

02 — Landscapes

Places that
define Asia.

The Himalaya

Earth’s highest range, a “third pole” of ice that feeds major rivers across the continent.

Lake Baikal

The deepest lake on Earth at about 1,640 m, holding roughly a fifth of the world’s unfrozen fresh water.

Mount Fuji

Japan’s sacred volcano, rising 3,776 m and shaping art, poetry and pilgrimage for centuries.

03 — Wildlife

Life on
the edge.

Vulnerable

Giant panda

Bamboo-forest protection in China has helped numbers climb to about 1,800 in the wild.

Endangered

Tiger

Around 4,500 tigers remain in the wild, in forests from India to the Russian Far East.

Critically endangered

Sumatran orangutan

Only around 14,000 remain as rainforest is cleared for plantations.

“Half of humanity, a thousand ways of living.”

04 — Challenges

What is
at stake.

01

Air pollution

Several of the world’s most polluted cities are in South Asia, affecting the health of millions.

02

Melting glaciers

Nearly two billion people depend on rivers fed by Himalayan snow and ice.

03

Forest loss

Palm oil, logging and farming are rapidly shrinking Southeast Asia’s rainforests.
