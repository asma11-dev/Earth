01 — Overview

Marine Life, in
a nutshell.

The ocean covers about 71% of Earth and holds roughly 97% of its water. More than 230,000 marine species are known to science, and many more are still undiscovered. Phytoplankton alone produce at least half of the oxygen we breathe.

The foundation

Plankton

Microscopic algae and animals form the base of the food web. Phytoplankton make at least half of Earth’s oxygen.

Over 30,000 species

Fish

From sardine schools of millions to tuna that cross entire oceans.

About 130 species

Marine mammals

Whales, dolphins, seals and manatees. Blue whales are the largest animals ever known.

Over 1,100 species

Sharks & rays

Ancient predators that keep ecosystems balanced. More than a third now face extinction risk.

Ink, brains & camouflage

Cephalopods

Octopus, squid and cuttlefish are intelligent invertebrates that can change colour in a blink.

03 — Spotlight

Giants in
trouble.

Endangered

Blue whale

Up to about 30 m long, recovering slowly after industrial whaling nearly wiped it out.

Vulnerable

Leatherback turtle

The largest sea turtle crosses entire oceans and can dive well over 1,000 m.

Endangered

Giant manta ray

Gentle filter feeders with wingspans over 5 m, caught by fisheries for their gill plates.

“We know the surface of the Moon better than the floor of our own ocean.”

04 — Pressures

What is
changing.

01

Overfishing

More than a third of assessed fish stocks are fished beyond sustainable limits.

02

Warming & less oxygen

Hotter water holds less oxygen, pushing species toward the poles and deeper waters.

03

Acidification

The ocean has absorbed a large share of our CO₂, becoming about 30% more acidic since industrial times.
