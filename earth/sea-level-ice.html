01 — Overview

Sea Level & Ice, in
a nutshell.

Global sea level has risen about 21–24 cm since 1880 and the rise is speeding up. Melting ice sheets and glaciers, plus warmer water expanding, are pushing the sea higher.

02 — Sources

Where the
water comes from.

Greenland & Antarctica

Ice sheets

Together they hold enough ice to raise the sea by about 65 m, so even small losses matter.

Slow rivers of ice

Mountain glaciers

Melting glaciers feed rivers and add water to the sea.

Warm water takes space

Thermal expansion

As oceans warm, the water itself expands and raises sea level.

Reflective shield

Sea ice

Floating ice does not raise sea level when it melts, but it reflects sunlight and cools the planet.

03 — Spotlight

Places on
the front line.

Low-lying

Pacific atolls

Nations like Tuvalu and Kiribati have land only a few metres above the sea.

Sinking + rising

Delta cities

Jakarta, Dhaka and Bangkok face rising seas and land that is subsiding.

Vanishing

Alpine glaciers

Swiss glaciers lost about 10% of their volume in just two years (2022–2023).

“Ice remembers every degree.”
